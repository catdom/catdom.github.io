The caption gets a face

Today the hero opens with one mono line: Jordi Catalán · Barcelona. Splitting it in two puts the name on its own line and turns the city into what it actually is — a second-order fact — and a mark to the left gives the pair something to sit against. Four ways to draw that mark, each on the hero's own ground with the claim underneath, since the caption is only ever read against it.

There is no photograph in the repository, so three of these draw the mark from something the site already owns. The fourth is the slot a real portrait would drop into.

Option A

The account row

The convention borrowed straight: a round monogram in the accent, a green dot for availability. Most familiar, and the least like the rest of the page — nothing else here is a circle, and nothing else is filled.

What I would ship

B now, D the day there is a photo

B is the one that belongs to this site. Every other block on the page is a square with one hairline and no fill; a round filled avatar with a status dot is a different design language wearing the same palette, and it will read as borrowed. B says the same thing — this is a person, here is where they are — in the vocabulary the page already speaks, and it earns the second line by putting the job title there rather than repeating the city.

D beats all three the moment a portrait exists, and I would keep B's frame for it: square-cropped, one hairline, same 44px. Send a photo and it is a ten-minute change.

One caution on A: the green dot is a claim about availability that nothing else on the site backs up, and a status light that is always on stops meaning anything. If availability is worth saying, the footer already says it in words.
